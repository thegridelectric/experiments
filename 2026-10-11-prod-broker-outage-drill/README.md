# prod-broker-outage-drill, 2026-10-11

> What this is: a planned outage of the prod broker (`hw1-1`), its
> listeners down for 9 minutes 20 seconds, to time how fast the
> alerter's prober notices a real broker outage and how fast the fleet
> reconnects. Verdict: Pass on the prober's claim; the reconnect timing
> is a finding.

## Why

The alerter's prober (`gwalerter probe` on the alerts box) is the one
detector that does not depend on the broker, and its broker-down path had
only been witnessed on the laptop against the dev broker
(`2026-10-07-alerter-broker-down/`). By its spec it raises
`BrokerUnreachable` once both doors have failed three probes running
while the fleet is unheard: about 3 to 4 minutes. This run checks that
against prod, and records how long each client takes to come back.

## Setup

- **Stop and start** on `hw1-1` over ssh as `ubuntu`:
  `cd ~/rmq-docker && sudo docker compose stop`, then `start`. FIS kept
  running. Nothing else was touched.
- **Detector under test:** `alerter-probe` and `alerter-rabbit` on the
  alerts box (gridworks-alerter `d61c4dd`). `alerter-tap` is disabled
  during the shadow week, so the alert was read from the alerter's store,
  not from Opsgenie.
- **Safety:** the houses keep their reports and send them when the broker
  returns; gwalert (journal-DB alerting, 10-minute silence threshold) was
  left running. LTN dispatch was unavailable for the outage.
- **Clients on the broker before the stop** (`rabbitmqctl` over ssh):
  11 MQTT (scadas beech, oak, fir, maple, spruce; LTNs beech, oak, fir,
  maple, elm, spruce; elm's scada was already unreachable) and 8 AMQP
  (weather, gnr, gnr-ear, ear, gjk, alerter, shovel, and the web-api2
  gateway on the password login `smqPublic`).

## Found

1. **PASS: the prober raised `BrokerUnreachable` on both doors (5671 and
   8883) 3 min 36 s after the listeners stopped**, inside the spec's 3 to
   4 minutes, and resolved both 17 s after the broker's startup completed,
   on its first probe. No `NoData` was raised during the outage: the
   open `BrokerUnreachable` held it, as specified.
2. **The AMQP clients were back 0.3 to 30 s after startup** (gnr,
   gnr-ear, ear, weather within 2 s; web-api2 5 s; gjk, alerter, shovel
   25–30 s).
3. **The MQTT clients waited 46 to 49 s after a broker that was already
   up**, and arrived in lockstep. The MQTT TLS listener started at
   18:27:25 and accepted the prober's MQTT check at 18:27:44, with no
   refusals in the log, yet all six LTNs connected at 18:28:12 within
   42 ms of each other and four scadas within 2.2 s after them, from five
   different networks. Oak's scada came at 18:29:04 (99 s).
4. **The timing fits exponential reconnect backoff from 1 s doubling to a
   120 s cap, all clients in phase** (the paho-mqtt default:
   1+2+4+8+16+32+64 = 127 s, then 120 s steps; 18:18:05 + 607 s =
   18:28:12). Not yet confirmed in the scada and LTN code. Consequences:
   after an outage longer than about two minutes the MQTT fleet takes up
   to 120 s to return after the broker does, and every client retries at
   the same instant, a reconnect storm through the FIS gate that at 100
   homes is about 200 logins inside a second.

## Analysis notes

- Client identities in the evidence are certificate CNs (GNodeIds or
  service uuids); `gridworks-infra/authority/cert-inventory.md` maps them
  to names. MQTT client ids are the clients' instance ids and were the
  same before and after: each reconnect lease-matched at the gate.
- The AMQP `connected_at` values are the broker's; the MQTT times come
  from the broker log's accept lines (`list_mqtt_connections` has no
  connect time).
- Durations are measured on the broker's own clock, from its log:
  detection from the listeners stopping (18:18:05.2), reconnects from
  "Server startup complete" (18:27:25.7). `views.py` computes both.
- The paho backoff reading is an inference from timing alone. Oak's 101 s
  is unexplained (a probable missed retry at 18:28:12; its log was not
  read).

## Folder contents & experimental method

**How the data was obtained.** Everything here was GENERATED by this
run: the experiment stopped the prod broker on purpose, and the evidence
was read off the live systems by hand over ssh during and right after
it. None of it is in the journal DB or the S3 eventstore; a re-run
produces a new dataset.

- `runbook.sh`: the drill one step per call (`census`, `stop`, `start`,
  `collect`); `stop` asks for confirmation. `collect` writes down the
  evidence reads that were done by hand.
- `evidence/alerter-store-alerts.json`: EXTERNAL EVIDENCE, the two
  `alerts` rows (raised and resolved `gw.alert` payloads) from the
  alerter's store `~/.local/share/gridworks/alerter/alerter.sqlite` on the
  alerts box, read-only, rows raised 18:16–18:30Z.
- `evidence/broker-log-reduced.txt`: EXTERNAL EVIDENCE, `docker logs
  rmq1` on hw1-1 for 18:17:30–18:29:30Z, reduced to stop/start, listener
  and connection-accept lines, public IPs replaced by stable tokens
  because this repo is public (provenance header in the file).
- `evidence/amqp-connections-after.tsv`, `evidence/mqtt-connections-after.tsv`:
  EXTERNAL EVIDENCE, `rabbitmqctl list_connections -q user protocol
  connected_at` and `list_mqtt_connections -q client_id user` on hw1-1
  after the fleet was back.
- `emit_instances.py`: builds `instances/` from the evidence.
- `instances/hw1.alerts-hw1.1.electricity.works.<door>.<state>-gw.alert-000.json`:
  the Firing and Resolved alert per door.
- `instances/gw.experiment.run-001.json`: the run window (the broker's
  SIGTERM to the last MQTT accept, both from the broker log), the verdict
  and the claim it stamps.
- `views.py`: the display CSVs below. The reconnect rows are a typed
  record inside the script, not an instance: no sema word covers a
  broker-side connection accept yet.

Regenerate the instances from the evidence (the evidence itself cannot
be regenerated; a new drill with `runbook.sh` makes a new dataset):

    uv run python emit_instances.py

The alert transitions, one row per `gw.alert` instance, with seconds
after the listeners stopped (`alerts-display.csv`):

    uv run python views.py alerts

Every connection accepted after startup, with its login and whether it
was held afterwards (`reconnects-display.csv`):

    uv run python views.py reconnects
